# Centenarian Support

Need help, found a bug, or want to suggest something? Email
[teeksone.bne@gmail.com](mailto:teeksone.bne@gmail.com). Please include your
iPhone model, iOS version and the app version (shown at the bottom of Settings
in the app).

## Common questions

**Do I need an account?**
No. There is no sign-in. Everything is stored on your iPhone.

**Will my habits move to a new iPhone?**
Yes, if you set up the new phone from an iCloud or computer backup of the old
one. Centenarian does not yet sync between devices on its own.

**I deleted the app. Can I get my history back?**
Only by restoring your iPhone from a backup made before you deleted it.
Because the app has no server, there is no other copy.

**How do I connect my Apple Watch?**
Open Progress → Vitals and choose to connect Apple Health. Centenarian reads
what your watch has saved to Apple Health; it does not talk to the watch
directly. Other wearables work too if they write to Apple Health.

**A habit didn't tick itself off.**
Automatic ticking needs Apple Health access for that type of data, and the
reading has to have reached Apple Health. Open the Health app to check the
reading is there, then reopen Centenarian. You can always tick a habit by hand.

**How do I stop the app reading my health data?**
In the Health app, go to Sharing → Apps → Centenarian and turn the categories
off.

**How do I change or turn off the daily reminder?**
Tap the gear icon on the Today tab and change it under reminders.

**How do I erase everything?**
Tap the gear icon on the Today tab, then Reset all data. This cannot be undone.

**How is my "lifestyle age" worked out?**
It is an estimate based on how consistently you keep the habits you track
and, if you have connected Apple Health, your readings. It is a motivational
guide, not a clinical measurement.

## Not medical advice

Centenarian is an educational wellbeing app. It does not diagnose, treat or
prevent any condition, and its scores, ranges and estimates are simplified
guides drawn from published population research. Talk to a doctor before
making significant changes to your exercise, diet or alcohol intake, and about
any reading or symptom that worries you.
